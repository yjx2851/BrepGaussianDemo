Abstract

Recovering editable CAD structure from ordinary images.

B-Rep models describe a 3D solid through explicit trimmed faces, edges, and corners. BrepGaussian tackles the challenge of recovering this structured representation directly from multi-view images, avoiding dependence on dense and clean point-cloud input.

The framework learns 3D parametric representations from 2D images with a Gaussian Splatting renderer and learnable features. A two-stage learning design first captures geometry and edges, then refines patch features for clean geometry and coherent instance representations.

Experiments in the paper report superior performance against recent state-of-the-art methods, moving image-based reconstruction closer to usable boundary representations.

Why it matters

BrepGaussian connects image-based neural rendering with explicit CAD recovery: the representation is learned from views, while the target output remains structured B-Rep rather than only a visual surface.

Method

A two-stage path from views to CAD boundaries.

The framework separates geometry reconstruction from feature refinement, making the learned representation useful for both shape recovery and patch-level organization.

Multi-view capture

Images provide the observation signal for reconstructing the object without relying on a clean scanned point cloud.

Geometry learning

A Gaussian Splatting renderer captures 3D shape structure and edge-aware cues from view supervision.

Patch refinement

Learnable features are optimized to form coherent surface instances that support downstream fitting.

B-Rep fitting

The reconstructed geometry and features are converted into explicit CAD boundaries and surface patches.
